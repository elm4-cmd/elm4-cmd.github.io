Privacy Policy for Word Meadow: Connect Puzzle

Effective date: 2026-10-15

P01 — Scope and publisher

This policy describes information handling for Word Meadow: Connect Puzzle for Android, version 1.2 (versionCode 5), and the older-version and earlier-data boundaries described below. The publisher is Emil Lindström, an individual established in Sweden who publishes under the name elm_cr4. The publisher is the controller where he determines the purposes and means of personal-data processing. Contact for privacy and support: support.elmcr4@gmail.com.

P02 — Information stored on your device

No Word Meadow account is required and the publisher provides no cloud sync. The app stores English and Swedish progress and saved game sessions, found target and bonus words, wheel order and revealed hints. Shared values include coins, hint and gameplay-time statistics, purchase grants and Remove Ads ownership. The app also stores language/sound/haptic preferences, tutorial and review flags and advertising timing.

An older Usage Analytics preference may remain in local data or a backup. Version 1.2 does not use that preference to enable Firebase Analytics. Removing the SDK does not itself erase older SDK-owned files or data already received by providers.

The app's purchase model stores purchase token, product, state, quantity, purchase time and acknowledgement information. Its local ledger also stores grant/finalization status, processing time and delivery source. Local technical logs can contain level IDs, status and SDK errors. The publisher has no app backend that remotely reads the game database and no publisher-operated remote log uploader. These local values support saving/resuming gameplay, the chosen features and purchase delivery; their purposes and retention are set out below.

Clearing Android app data can erase progress, coins, preferences and local purchase records. Uninstalling may remove local data, subject to Android's behavior. These actions do not themselves delete earlier data held by providers or Google purchase history, and do not guarantee that a backup cannot restore data.

P03 — Android backup and transfer

Word Meadow permits Android app-data backup. Depending on the device, platform settings and transport, databases, preferences and app files may be copied to a backup service or transferred between devices. This is separate from a Word Meadow cloud account. Storage, access and deletion depend on the actual service; removing local app data does not necessarily delete existing backup copies.

P04 — Advertising and advertising choices

Google Mobile Ads (AdMob) provides eligible interstitial ads between levels and optional rewarded ads that you request. Remove Ads suppresses mandatory interstitials; rewarded ads remain voluntary. Remove Ads is a purchase choice and does not promise that all Google SDKs stop operating.

When requests are eligible, the advertising SDK can initialize and preload rewarded ads before you press the rewarded-ad button, including when you own Remove Ads. Voluntary viewing is therefore separate from any data processing needed to request and preload an eligible ad.

Google UMP obtains and manages applicable advertising/privacy choices. The app uses UMP's request-eligibility state. When UMP requires the privacy entry point, Settings provides Privacy Choices to reopen its form. The app responds to the privacy-form state by pausing new ad requests and show eligibility and clearing preloaded ads after an updated choice. It rejects old loading results. An already showing ad is not recalled. These controls do not themselves delete provider data.

Google's information for the advertising services describes IP/network and device information, approximate location, advertising and service-generated identifiers, ad interactions and diagnostic information. For Mobile Ads 25.4.0, Google Support describes Android advertising ID (AAID), SDK Instance ID and Publisher First-Party ID. App Set ID is a separate identifier described in the existing technical service material. These identifiers are distinct; the app supplies no app-account/user ID or publisher-provided identifier. A publisher first-party identifier is SDK-managed and is described by Google as enabled by default, subject to applicable privacy choices and configuration.

Available identifiers, fields and advertising mode depend on device settings, region, choices and service configuration. This is provider-described processing potential, not a promise that every field is sent in every request or a complete per-mode payload list. The app does not provide a Word Meadow account.

UMP's consent-update request can include app and SDK version, package/app name, AdMob application ID, device OS/model/API, language, screen information, under-age-of-consent flag and selected stored consent/privacy signals. UMP stores consent/privacy-option status and applicable technical consent strings locally. Fields depend on the request and configuration; Word Meadow supplies no consent sync identifier. These requests also expose network IP to the receiving service. Non-personalized or limited ads can still involve personal data. Google privacy information.

Advertising partners can participate without additional mediation SDKs in the app. Applicable partner information and privacy links are supplied through the Google consent service where its form is used. No fixed number of legal recipients or Google-only advertising configuration is promised. Each participating service has its own processing purposes and privacy information; a partner catalogue is not a statement that every listed partner receives your information on every ad request.

P05 — Purchases and Google Play

Google Play handles payment and its account/order/transaction data under its own applicable terms. Word Meadow does not receive raw card details. The app's limited purchase model and ledger support delivery, avoiding duplicate grants, acknowledgement/consumption, retries and ownership reconciliation. Purchase tokens are used in Google Play acknowledgement and consumption calls. Ownership is queried on startup or return to the foreground as well as during purchases. Product information, offer tokens and formatted prices are also used in the shop and held in memory. The SDK's raw Purchase object can contain additional fields, including an order ID; these are not copied into the app's own purchase model.

Google Play In-App Review may display Google's rating/review interface. Any rating or text you submit is handled by Google Play. The API does not give the app the stars, review text or confirmation of submission. Google may publish a review or handle private test feedback under the applicable Play process.

P06 — Firebase Analytics in version 1.2

Version 1.2 removes the app's Firebase Analytics SDK implementation and does not send app-authored Firebase Analytics events. It has no Usage Analytics control. This does not disable advertising, consent, purchase or review services; those services can still process information as described in their sections. Advertising services may still measure ad usage and diagnose their operation.

P07 — Service administration and older versions

Developer account administration and communications with Google about the service are separate from your gameplay. Google's Firebase Service Data description concerns specified service/admin operations, separately from Customer Data. The project's extra Service Data-use setting does not by itself establish an additional player-data operation in version 1.2. Earlier Analytics player data is not described as Firebase Service Data merely because of that setting.

The last documented Google Play account scope distributed version 1.0/code4. The following older-version information applies where it remains distributed. Its frozen source includes a separate Usage Analytics choice with app and manifest defaults OFF. When enabled, the app uses Firebase Analytics for predefined usage and gameplay events; the manual event limits do not describe all SDK-generated information. The frozen source is not alone a verification of every field sent by the actually distributed artifact.

Google's Analytics disclosure describes an app-instance identifier, app/device, lifecycle and screen/session information, IP-derived coarse location, advertising ID subject to configuration, and automatic purchase events that can include product and price. Analytics app-instance ID and Firebase installation ID are different; a dependency alone does not establish that a Firebase installation ID is generated or transmitted. Google describes using IP to derive coarse location and discarding IP before Analytics storage; this still involves processing IP.

Account observations on 10 October 2026 show Google products/services sharing OFF, modeling/business insights, technical support and business recommendations ON, and a Firebase link with extended audience integration ON. These dated settings concern the relevant older-version/earlier-data operation; they do not establish an active Firebase Analytics operation in version 1.2 or replace a valid choice.

Crashlytics is removed from version 1.2. The verified frozen version 1.0 source has a separate Crash Reports choice, with app and manifest defaults OFF. If enabled, it uses Firebase Crashlytics to investigate crashes. App-authored non-fatal reports use a limited category/exception-type message; diagnostic context includes screen, chapter, level, session kind and build type. This does not mean all SDK-generated crash reports are sanitized or anonymous. Google's documentation describes crash traces, device information and installation identifiers for its separate Crashlytics processing.

Earlier provider data and SDK-owned local files are not erased by removing the SDK in 1.2. Google describes 90 days for covered crash traces/associated identifiers before starting removal from live and backup systems; this is not guaranteed total deletion on day 90.

P08 — Offline dictionaries

English validation uses SCOWL-derived word data and Swedish validation uses approved SALDO-derived data. Lookup is local without an online dictionary API. SCOWL and SALDO provide bundled content and are not runtime recipients of attempted words. Attribution and licenses appear in Third-Party Notices.

P11 — Retention and deletion

Local progress, preferences, statistics and purchase records remain in the installation until normal app operations replace/remove them or Android's controls remove app data. Completed sessions and purchase intents follow the app's existing completion/transition rules. Retained purchase records prevent duplicate delivery; there is no scheduled age-based cleanup of the ledger.

For still-distributed older versions and earlier Analytics data, the property observed on 10 October 2026 has event retention 2 months, user retention 14 months and reset-on-new-activity ON. Activity can extend identifier retention; aggregate reports do not all have the same limit. Removing Firebase Analytics from version 1.2 is not a request to delete earlier data from the provider's servers. The publisher has stated there are no separate Analytics exports or copies.

Ads/UMP/partners, Play/Payments/review, backup and hosting have separate operations and retention. A report window or vendor cookie duration is not a retention period for all data. The relevant providers' privacy information and controls describe their own retention and deletion; changing an app setting does not set a common retention period for every provider. For Google services, see Google's retention information.

For support/privacy correspondence, personal Gmail receives your email address, message, any attachments you choose to send and the app-version line in the draft. No automatic database, token or log attachment is sent. The publisher handles support and privacy correspondence and checks the support mailbox every weekday. We keep only the correspondence and attachments needed to handle your request. Unnecessary material is removed earlier. When the requested response or action is complete and no concrete follow-up remains, the case is closed and the relevant correspondence is deleted without unjustified delay. If an identified ongoing matter or applicable obligation requires further retention, only the necessary information is kept; its purpose and defined end condition are documented and reviewed. Gmail Trash and Google's own retention and deletion lifecycle are separate from our deletion action.
